# Three Digital Business Models, One Financial Lens

## Overview

This project compares the financial profiles of Microsoft, Adobe and Netflix through a common analytical framework focused on growth, profitability, cash generation and balance-sheet strength.

The objective is not to rank the companies or identify a universal “winner.” Instead, the analysis highlights how three successful digital businesses can produce materially different financial outcomes because of differences in scale, margins, reinvestment, cash generation and capital structure.

## Companies Analysed

- **Microsoft Corporation (MSFT)**
- **Adobe Inc. (ADBE)**
- **Netflix, Inc. (NFLX)**

## Key Findings

- **Microsoft** combines the largest revenue base, the strongest latest revenue growth, the highest operating and net margins, and the strongest balance-sheet flexibility.
- **Adobe** records the highest latest free cash flow margin and maintains strong cash generation.
- **Netflix** shows the largest improvement in both operating margin and free cash flow margin over the common historical period.

The analysis therefore identifies trade-offs rather than producing a composite score or overall ranking.

## Methodology

FY2021–FY2025 is used as the common historical comparison window for trend analysis.

Revenue is indexed to FY2021 = 100 to allow more meaningful comparison across companies with materially different absolute scale.

Free cash flow is defined as:

**Operating Cash Flow - Capital Expenditure**

Latest/LTM metrics are also included, but these may correspond to different reporting dates across the three companies and should not be interpreted as a single-date snapshot.

Historical CAGR measures outside the common trend window may use different company-specific periods and are therefore not treated as directly comparable.

## Analytical Framework

The comparison focuses on four dimensions:

1. **Growth**
2. **Profitability**
3. **Cash Generation**
4. **Balance-Sheet Strength**

No composite score or overall ranking is produced because the companies operate materially different business models and several metrics are not economically equivalent across the peer set.
